Funeral Welcome Sign Template: What to Include, Sizes & Printing

A welcome sign sets a gentle tone as guests arrive. This guide covers what to write, which sizes work for easels or foam-board mounting, and how to personalize and print a matching template in minutes using free software.

What is a funeral welcome sign and why use one?

A funeral welcome sign sits at the entrance of the service venue — on an easel, a stand, or mounted on foam board — and greets attendees with the loved one's name, dates, and a short message. It helps guests feel oriented and welcomed before they take a seat.

Unlike the folded program, which goes home with each person, the welcome sign stays at the venue. It works for traditional funerals, memorial services, and celebrations of life alike.

  • Shows the full name, birth and passing dates
  • Adds a brief phrase such as "Celebrating the Life of…" or "In Loving Memory"
  • Matches the visual theme of the program and memory cards for a cohesive look

What to write on a funeral welcome sign

Keep the text short so it reads clearly from a few feet away. Include the essentials first, then add one personal line if space allows.

Standard layout: full legal name (or the name they went by), birth year – passing year, service title ("Funeral Service," "Memorial Service," "Celebration of Life"), date and time of the service, and a short welcome line.

  • "Welcome to the Celebration of Life for Maria Elena Rossi"
  • "In Loving Memory of James T. Chen, 1952–2026"
  • "Please join us in remembering Sarah — Service begins at 2:00 PM"

Standard sizes and aspect ratios for printing

Most print shops and home printers handle a few common poster sizes. The 2:3 aspect ratio (24×36 in, 16×24 in, 12×18 in) scales cleanly without cropping. A 24×36 in sign is large enough for a chapel entrance; 16×24 in fits a tabletop easel; 12×18 in works for a smaller foyer.

If you design in a 2:3 ratio, you can print any of these three sizes from the same file — just choose the paper size at checkout.

  • 24×36 in — full easel sign, visible across a lobby
  • 16×24 in — mid-size, easy to transport
  • 12×18 in — compact, fits a small stand or table

How to personalize and print a welcome sign template

Open the fill-in PDF in the free Adobe Acrobat Reader app on your phone, tablet, or computer. Tap each placeholder — name, dates, service title, time — and type your text. Save the file. No design software, account, or subscription is needed.

Photos can be added on a computer only (drag and drop into the image frame). On a phone you can edit text but not insert images.

  • Download the PDF instantly after purchase
  • Open in Adobe Acrobat Reader (free, pre-installed on most devices)
  • Tap sample text, type your details, save
  • Print at home on heavyweight matte cardstock or send to Staples, Office Depot, or FedEx Office for same-day pickup
  • Ask the shop to mount on foam board for rigidity on an easel

Printing at home vs. a local print shop

Home printing works if you have a printer that takes 11×17 in or 12×18 in paper and you're comfortable trimming. For 16×24 in or 24×36 in, a local shop is faster and cheaper than large-format inkjet ink. Most shops accept a PDF on a USB drive or emailed file and can mount on 3/16 in foam board while you wait.

Call ahead to confirm same-day turnaround and foam-board mounting; some locations need a few hours during peak times.

  • Home: 12×18 in on 65–80 lb matte cardstock, trim to size
  • Shop: 16×24 in or 24×36 in on matte poster paper + foam board mount
  • Bring the saved PDF on phone or USB; no account needed at the counter

Questions

Can I edit the welcome sign on my phone?

Yes. Open the PDF in the free Adobe Acrobat Reader app, tap the placeholder text, type your details, and save. Photo insertion works on a computer only.

What paper weight should I use for a foam-board mounted sign?

Ask the print shop for matte poster paper (typically 8–10 mil). They will spray-mount it to 3/16 in foam board so it stands rigid on an easel without curling.

Does the template include bleed for edge-to-edge printing?

The welcome sign file is set up at the exact trim size (24×36 in, 2:3 ratio). Most shops print with a small white border; if you need full bleed, ask them to print on 24.25×36.25 in and trim.

Can I use the same template for a celebration of life instead of a funeral?

Absolutely. Change the service title line to "Celebration of Life" or "Memorial Gathering" — the layout and botanical design work for any tone.
