What to Write in a Funeral Program: Content Ideas for Each Panel

A funeral program guides guests through the service and becomes a keepsake. Most programs have four panels: cover, order of service, life story, and thank-you. Below are simple prompts and examples for each section so you can write something personal without starting from a blank page.

Cover panel: name, dates, and a welcoming line

The cover sets the tone. Include the full name your person was known by, their birth and death dates, and a short phrase — a favorite saying, a scripture line, or simply "In Loving Memory." A photo works well here; choose one that feels like them. If you use the Soft Botanical suite, the cover is the first page of the folded booklet; tap the placeholder in Adobe Acrobat Reader on your phone or computer, type, and insert the photo (photos can be added on a computer). Save and you're ready to print.

  • Full name (include maiden name or nickname if meaningful)
  • Birth date – death date
  • One short line: "Forever in our hearts" | "A life well lived" | Psalm 23:1
  • Photo: portrait or a candid moment that makes you smile

Order of service: the roadmap for the hour

List each element in the order it happens so guests know what to expect. Keep wording simple: "Prelude music," "Welcome and opening prayer," "Eulogy by [name]," "Hymn: Amazing Grace," "Closing remarks." Add the officiant's name and any readers or musicians. If the service includes communion, a candle lighting, or a military honor guard, note those too. This panel becomes the schedule everyone follows.

  • Prelude / gathering music
  • Welcome and opening words (name the officiant)
  • Readings, poems, or scripture (list reader names)
  • Eulogy or life tribute (speaker name)
  • Music selections (song titles, soloist or congregational)
  • Closing prayer or blessing
  • Recessional music and exit instructions

Life story panel: a snapshot, not a biography

You don't need a full obituary here. Write 150–250 words that capture personality: where they grew up, work or passion, marriage and family, hobbies, and one or two defining traits. Use bullet points for milestones if paragraphs feel overwhelming. The Soft Botanical suite's life-story panel is the third page of the folded program; tap the sample text and type directly in the free Adobe Reader app on your phone — no design skills needed.

  • Birthplace, parents, siblings
  • Education, career, military service
  • Marriage date and spouse's name, children and grandchildren
  • Passions: gardening, coaching, quilting, Sunday crosswords
  • One sentence that sums them up: "She never missed a school play."

Thank-you and reception panel: practical details with warmth

Thank the people who helped — hospice staff, pallbearers, the choir, the friend who brought lasagna every Tuesday. Then give clear reception details: location, address, start time, and whether it's a meal or light refreshments. If there's a graveside service afterward, list that too. This panel turns gratitude into action so guests know where to go next.

  • "With gratitude to Dr. Patel and the team at Mercy Hospice…"
  • Names of pallbearers, honorary pallbearers, ushers
  • Reception: [Venue], [Address], [Time] — all welcome
  • Graveside: [Cemetery], [Time] — family and friends invited
  • Memorial donation note if preferred over flowers

Quick personalization checklist before you print

Open the PDF in Adobe Acrobat Reader (free on phone or computer). Tap each gray placeholder and type. On a computer you can also drag in photos. Check spelling, confirm dates and times with the officiant and venue, then save. Print a test page on plain paper to verify folds and alignment. When it looks right, print on 80–100 lb cardstock at home or send the PDF to Staples, Office Depot, or FedEx Office for same-day pickup — no account or shipping needed.

  • Verify every name spelling and date
  • Confirm service order with officiant
  • Print one test sheet on plain paper
  • Print final on cardstock (80–100 lb) or pick up same-day at a print shop
  • Fold landscape US Letter to 5.5 × 8.5 in booklet

Questions

How long should the life story section be?

Aim for 150–250 words — about three to five short paragraphs or a handful of bullet points. It's a snapshot, not a full obituary.

Can I write the program on my phone?

Yes. Open the fill-in PDF in the free Adobe Acrobat Reader app on iPhone or Android, tap each field, and type. Photos can be added on a computer only.

What if I don't know the exact order of service yet?

Start with a typical flow: music, welcome, readings, eulogy, music, closing. Fill in what you know; you can edit and re-save the PDF as details are confirmed.

Do I need special paper for the folded program?

Use 80–100 lb cardstock (often labeled "cover weight"). Standard US Letter (8.5 × 11 in) prints landscape and folds to a 5.5 × 8.5 in booklet.

Can the welcome sign be printed smaller?

Yes. The 24 × 36 in file uses a 2:3 ratio, so it also prints cleanly at 16 × 24 in or 12 × 18 in without any layout changes.
