### 1) **“The Moment When…” (timestamp POV narration)**
**What makes it work**
- Micro-story labeling: the viewer immediately knows “when” they’re about to watch.
- Great for binge-rewatch because the label feels like a “final answer” moment.
- Works with a 2–3 second hook + then a clean unbroken stretch.

**How MotionVault could use it (specific title)**
- **Title:** *Black Clover Season 2* (anime)
- **Edit idea:** Start on the instant a character commits to the move (mouth/eyes change + power-up).
- **Text overlay beats:**
  - 0:00–0:02: “**THE MOMENT WHEN HE REALIZED HE COULDN’T GO BACK**”
  - 0:02–0:18: continuous action stretch (power up → first impact)
  - last 2s: “**HE’S LOCKED IN.**”
- **Sound:** rising hit right as the power manifests.

**Source link**
- Example of the “moment lead” style in recent shorts: https://youtu.be/HTyCpjnKoak

**Fading?** *No clear fading signal*—this is still an evergreen conversion format.

### 2) **Velocity Cuts (beat-synced montage + hit-stops)**
**What makes it work**
- Fast pattern changes every beat: keeps retention high in the first 3 seconds.
- “Action grammar”: power up → clash → reaction → camera push → repeat.
- Even without dialogue, the edit reads.

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *Grand Theft Auto VI* (game footage)
- **Edit idea:** Use a 20–30 sec chase/combat chain:
  - Hook: “**HE THOUGHT HE’D GET AWAY…**”
  - Montage: 3 short action ramps (spot → chase acceleration → last-second escape fail)
  - Final beat: vehicle flips / last hit landing → text “**REWATCH THAT HIT**”
- Keep each source stretch short (2–4 sec) and cut on impact frames.

**Source link**
- Recent “high-energy” movie edit pattern: https://youtu.be/2ahMXLOOtag

**Fading?** *No*—velocity-style is still dominant, but it’s crowded. Needs extra clarity in the hook.

### 3) **POV Overlay (you are the character / you’re the victim / you’re the hunter)**
**What makes it work**
- Instant immersion. Comments often mirror the POV (“BRO I’D PANIC”).
- Works best when the POV text changes 2–3 times max (not constant clutter).

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *One Piece*
- **Edit idea:** “POV: YOU JUST WATCHED SOMEONE TURN THE WHOLE FIGHT AROUND”
  - 0:00–0:02: POV line
  - 0:02–0:20: single narrative run (preparation → reveal → first decisive strike)
  - final beat: close-up reaction + “**Now it’s your turn.**”
- Use bold caption with outline for readability on busy anime frames.

**Source link**
- POV-style recent example: https://youtu.be/f2FWvyPwhGU

**Fading?** *No*—POV remains consistent.

---

### 4) **Side-by-Side Comparison (A vs B, “who wins?”)**
**What makes it work**
- Viewers debate in comments (algorithm boost via engagement).
- Simple structure: setup → compare moments → final verdict line.

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *Black Clover* (Season 2)
- **Edit idea:** “**RANKING: WHO HITS HARDER?**” (Asta vs antagonist / two rivals)
- Layout:
  - Left: faster/aggressive moment
  - Right: cleaner/stronger moment
  - Text swaps at each hit: “**SPEED** / **POWER** / **CONTROL**”
  - Final screen: “**My pick: [name]**” (no need to be factual—make it opinionated)

**Source link**
- Comparison format is common in the recent “edit” ecosystem; one strong example category from the list: https://youtu.be/pPqBgUBG_2Q

**Fading?** *Slight risk of crowding* but still strong when the comparison is specific and visually clear.

### 5) **Before / After (transformation, consequence, “they changed forever”)**
**What makes it work**
- Clear contrast creates automatic comprehension.
- Perfect for tragedy-to-triumph, mindset shifts, or “decision moment.”

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *The Apothecary Diaries Season 3*
- **Edit idea:** “**BEFORE: CALM** → **AFTER: SHE KNOWS THE TRUTH**”
- Structure:
  - 0:00–0:10: calm/observing frames + text “Before”
  - 0:10–0:22: reveal/panic/resolve + text “After”
  - last 2s: single stare/decision face + “**No going back now.**”

**Source link**
- Transformation AMV/edit energy seen in recent shorts: https://youtu.be/vRWjJ-0vXkM

**Fading?** *No*—it’s still a timeless structure.

### 6) **Ranking Edit (Top 3 / Most ruthless / Best reactions)**
**What makes it work**
- List format improves scanning—viewers understand the “game” instantly.
- You can end with a “final boss” beat that drives rewatch.

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *Overgeared* (anime/game-ish fanbase)
- **Edit idea:** “**TOP 3 MOST ICONIC MOMENTS THIS ARC**”
- Pick 3 short clips (each 6–9 seconds max) in story order:
  1) power reveal
  2) “impossible” solve
  3) final kill / victory pose
- Final beat: “**#1 is the rewatch bait.**” (then show #1 again for 1–2 seconds)

**Source link**
- “Iconic moment” style edit category: https://youtu.be/qa5t8dp5gZc

**Fading?** *Not fading*, but it’s frequent—make it feel *specific* to the arc.

### 7) **Meme-Template Captioning (text-first comedy over serious footage)**
**What makes it work**
- Meme captions can carry the hook even if the scene is unknown to new viewers.
- Best when the joke lands in <2 seconds and stays readable.

**MotionVault example**
- **Title:** *Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2* (anime—based on your list; release details may be outdated, verify internally)
- **Template idea:** “**WHEN YOU SAID ‘ONE LAST JOB’…**”
- Show:
  - setup frames (walking/gear up)
  - cut to chaos (one hit / betrayal / explosion)
  - final beat: “**You’re cooked.**”
- Keep text minimal, big, and with a consistent meme font.

**Source link**
- Meme-caption vibe seen in recent Shorts: https://youtu.be/nUDG9g6aUq4

**Fading?** *Meme templates can fade fast if they’re overused*—use fresh phrasing + scene-specific humor.
