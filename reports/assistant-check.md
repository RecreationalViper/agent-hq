## ✅ What should I write in an obituary for my grandmother?
Writing an obituary for your grandmother is a tender way to honor her story. While requirements vary by publication, here is a simple structure to guide you:

- Full name (including maiden name if meaningful)
- Dates of birth and passing, and her hometown
- Survivors: a list of immediate family members who preceded her in death and those who survive her
- Life story: a few sentences about her career, passions, volunteer work, or favorite pastimes
- Service details: date, time, and location of the funeral or memorial service, or a note if private

## ✅ What's the difference between a funeral and a celebration of life?
A funeral is a formal ceremony that usually includes a viewing or visitation, a graveside or cremation service, and a closing ritual such as a eulogy, prayer, or music. It often follows a traditional order of service and may be more solemn.

A celebration of life is a more informal, upbeat gathering that focuses on honoring the person’s memories, achievements, and personality. It can be held before or after burial or cremation, may include stories, photos, music, and personal readings, and is often less structured than a funeral. Both serve the same purpose of honoring the deceased, but the tone and format differ.

## ✅ How much does cremation usually cost?
In the United States, direct (unattended) cremation usually costs between $1,000 and $3,000. This typically includes the transport of the body, the basic services of a funeral director, the cremation process itself, and a temporary container for the ashes.

A traditional viewing followed by cremation with a rental casket and service usually ranges from $4,000 to $8,000 or more. 

Keep in mind that prices vary significantly by region and individual provider. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, funeral homes are required to give you itemized price lists over the phone or in person, so it is always wise to compare options. Additional costs like death certificates, obituaries, urns, or cemetery plots for the ashes are usually extra.

## ✅ What does a pastor usually say at a graveside service?
A graveside or committal service is usually short—around 15 to 20 minutes—and focuses on comfort, final farewells, and returning the body to the earth. 

A pastor, priest, or celebrant generally follows this gentle structure:

- Opening words: A brief welcome and a scripture reading or poem about comfort, peace, or eternal life.
- Reflection or prayer: A short thought honoring the person's life and faith, followed by a prayer of thanksgiving and comfort for the grieving family.
- Committal: The central moment. The pastor speaks words of release, often using traditional phrases like "earth to earth, ashes to ashes, dust to dust," committing the body to its resting place.
- Final blessing: A closing benediction or blessing of peace for everyone gathered.

Often, guests are then invited to place a flower, a handful of earth, or a stone on the casket or urn as a personal goodbye.
