## ✅ What's the difference between a funeral and a celebration of life?
A funeral is usually a more formal, often religious service that focuses on mourning, prayer, and rites such as a burial or cremation. It often follows traditional customs and may include a clergy‑led ceremony, hymns, scripture readings and a graveside committal.

A celebration of life is a less formal gathering that emphasizes remembering the person’s personality, passions, and happy moments. It can be held anywhere (home, park, community hall) and may include music the loved one enjoyed, stories, photos, food, and activities that reflect their life. The tone is often uplifting rather than solemn, though grief is still honored.

Both can include similar elements—eulogies, photos, music, and a moment to say goodbye—so families often blend them to fit their wishes. If you’re planning either, you can use a simple printable program or welcome sign from Forever Held to help guide guests and create a gentle keepsake. ## ✅ How much does cremation usually cost?
Cremation costs vary a lot by location, the funeral home you choose, and what services are included. In the United States you’ll typically see these ranges:

- Direct cremation (no ceremony, just the cremation and a simple container): $600 – $1,500.
- Cremation with a basic memorial service or viewing: $1,200 – $3,000.
- Full-service cremation (including a ceremony, urn, and possibly a reception): $2,500 – $5,000.

Prices can be higher in big cities or lower in rural areas. The FTC Funeral Rule requires funeral homes to give you an itemized price list, so you can compare exactly what’s included (cremation fee, transportation, paperwork, urn, etc.). It’s a good idea to ask for the written list and to check if any discounts are available for veterans, seniors, or prepaid plans. If you have a specific provider in mind, ask them to break down the costs so you can see what fits your budget.
